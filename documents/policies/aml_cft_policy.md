# Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorist Financing Policy

> SYNTHETIC DOCUMENT. Lagoon Bank Plc is a fictional institution. This policy was written for the
> development and evaluation of FIRA and does not reproduce or summarise any real law or regulation.
> Institutions must ingest their own approved policies and the regulatory texts that apply to them.

## 1. Purpose

This policy sets out how Lagoon Bank Plc ("the Bank") prevents its products and services from being used to
launder the proceeds of crime or to finance terrorism. It establishes minimum standards for customer due
diligence, transaction monitoring, investigation, escalation and record keeping.

## 2. Scope

The policy applies to all customers, accounts, products, channels and staff of the Bank, including digital
channels (mobile and web banking), card payments, branch and ATM transactions, and transfers to and from other
institutions.

## 3. Risk-Based Approach

3.1 The Bank applies a risk-based approach. Customers, products, channels and geographies are rated low, medium
or high risk, and controls are proportionate to the assessed risk.

3.2 Risk ratings are reviewed when material new information arises, including when transaction monitoring
identifies behaviour inconsistent with the customer's profile.

3.3 Automated risk scores are decision-support tools. A risk score alone is never sufficient to conclude that a
customer is engaged in money laundering, and no adverse action may be taken on the basis of a score alone.

## 4. Transaction Monitoring

4.1 All customer activity is subject to automated transaction monitoring. Monitoring scenarios must be
documented, tested and periodically tuned.

4.2 Monitoring must consider, at a minimum: the velocity and volume of transactions compared with the customer's
own history and peer group; unusually large transactions; rapid movement of funds through an account; receipt
of funds from many unrelated parties; circular movements of funds; activity on previously dormant accounts;
payments to high-risk merchant categories; use of new or shared devices; and transactions in geographies that
are unusual for the customer.

4.3 Alerts must be triaged within five business days and either closed with a documented rationale or escalated
to an investigation.

## 5. Red Flag Indicators

The following indicators require further review. They are indicators, not proof of wrongdoing:

- **Rapid pass-through of funds:** credits are followed shortly by debits of a similar total value, leaving a
  low residual balance, particularly when the counterparties are unrelated.
- **Fan-in from multiple senders:** an account receives transfers from an unusually large number of different
  senders over a short period, inconsistent with its stated purpose.
- **Circular transfers:** funds leave an account and return to it, directly or through intermediaries, within
  a short period and with similar amounts, which may indicate layering or artificial turnover.
- **Dormant account reactivation:** an account with no activity for an extended period suddenly receives or
  sends significant value.
- **Structuring:** multiple transactions just below internal or regulatory reporting thresholds.
- **Shared identifiers or devices:** several apparently unrelated customers share a device, phone number, email
  address or residential address.
- **Unusual geography:** activity in countries not associated with the customer, or physically impossible
  sequences of card-present transactions.
- **High-risk merchant activity:** repeated or escalating payments to gambling, crypto-asset exchange,
  money-transfer or high-value goods merchants inconsistent with the customer's profile.

## 6. Investigation and Escalation

6.1 Investigations are conducted by trained analysts. Investigators must document the evidence reviewed, the
analysis performed, alternative legitimate explanations considered, and the conclusion reached.

6.2 Where an investigation concludes that there are reasonable grounds to suspect money laundering or terrorist
financing, the case must be escalated to the MLRO. Only the MLRO, or a delegate authorised in writing, may decide
to file a suspicious transaction report with the competent authority.

6.3 Decisions to restrict, freeze or close an account, or to exit a customer relationship, require approval by
an authorised officer and must never be made automatically by a system.

6.4 Staff must not disclose to a customer or third party that an investigation is under way or that a report
has been or may be filed (tipping off).

## 7. Use of Automated and AI-Assisted Tools

7.1 Automated tools, including machine-learning models and AI assistants, may be used to prioritise alerts,
collect evidence and draft investigation summaries.

7.2 Every conclusion drafted by an automated tool must cite the underlying evidence and must be reviewed and
approved by a human investigator before it is relied upon.

7.3 Automated tools must not describe a customer as a criminal or state that an offence has occurred.
Language must remain factual and neutral, for example "activity consistent with the rapid pass-through
indicator" rather than "the customer is laundering money".

## 8. Record Keeping

Investigation records, the evidence reviewed and the decisions taken must be retained for at least the period
set out in the Data Retention Standard and must be retrievable for audit and supervisory review.

## 9. Governance

This policy is owned by the MLRO, reviewed at least annually, and approved by the Board Risk Committee.
