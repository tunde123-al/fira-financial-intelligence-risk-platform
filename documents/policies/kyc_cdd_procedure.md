# Know Your Customer and Customer Due Diligence Procedure

> SYNTHETIC DOCUMENT for FIRA development. Lagoon Bank Plc is fictional. Not legal or regulatory guidance.

## 1. Objective

This procedure describes how the Bank identifies and verifies customers, understands the purpose and intended
nature of the relationship, and keeps customer information up to date.

## 2. Customer Identification

2.1 Individual customers must provide verified identity information and at least one verified contact channel
(phone number or email address) and a residential address.

2.2 Business customers must provide registration information, the identity of directors and of beneficial
owners above the ownership threshold defined in the Beneficial Ownership Standard.

2.3 Identity documents may be captured as images. Image capture must be retained only for as long as required
by the Data Retention Standard.

## 3. KYC Levels

- **Level 1:** basic identification; low transaction limits.
- **Level 2:** verified identity and address; standard limits.
- **Level 3:** full verification including source of funds for high-value customers.

## 4. Enhanced Due Diligence (EDD)

4.1 EDD is required for customers rated high risk, for politically exposed persons, and when monitoring
identifies behaviour that materially deviates from the customer's expected profile.

4.2 EDD triggers identified through monitoring include:

- several customers registered with the **same phone number, email address or device**, where no family or
  business relationship has been established;
- account access from a **new device and a new country at the same time**, followed by high-value transfers;
- turnover materially above the level declared at onboarding;
- repeated **cross-border activity** inconsistent with the customer's stated occupation or business.

4.3 EDD measures include obtaining additional information on source of funds and source of wealth, verifying
the relationship between connected customers, and senior management approval to continue the relationship.

## 5. Shared Identifiers and Households

5.1 Members of the same household commonly share an address and sometimes a device. A shared address between two
customers is therefore not, on its own, an indicator of suspicion.

5.2 A device, phone number or email address shared by **three or more** unrelated customers should be reviewed,
because mule networks are frequently operated from a small number of controlled devices and contact details.

## 6. Periodic Review

High-risk customers are reviewed every 12 months, medium-risk every 24 months and low-risk every 36 months, or
earlier when a trigger event occurs.

## 7. Customer Contact During Investigations

Requests for information from a customer during an investigation must follow approved scripts so that the
customer is not alerted to the existence of a suspicion (see the AML/CFT Policy, section 6.4).
