# Transaction Monitoring and Alert Investigation Procedure

> SYNTHETIC DOCUMENT for FIRA development. Lagoon Bank Plc is fictional.

## 1. Alert Lifecycle

1. An alert is generated by a monitoring scenario or a risk score above the investigation threshold.
2. A Level 1 analyst triages the alert within five business days.
3. Alerts that cannot be closed at triage are opened as investigations.
4. Investigations are concluded as *legitimate*, *insufficient evidence*, *confirmed suspicious* or *escalated*.
5. Confirmed suspicious and escalated cases are referred to the MLRO.

## 2. Behavioural Baselines

2.1 Deviation scenarios compare the investigation window (normally the last 30 days) with the customer's own
baseline (normally the preceding 90 days) and with the peer group defined by customer segment.

2.2 Where the baseline contains fewer than five outbound transactions, deviation results must be treated as low
confidence and the investigator must rely on peer comparisons and other evidence.

## 3. Scenario Guidance

### 3.1 Velocity and bursts

A burst of transactions within one hour, particularly many small card-not-present payments with a high share of
failed authorisations, is consistent with card testing. A sustained increase in daily transaction counts should
be compared with seasonal and business explanations before escalation.

### 3.2 Amount deviation

A single large payment is common and frequently legitimate (property deposits, school fees, travel). Investigators
should check the merchant category, the counterparty history and whether the payment was made from the customer's
usual device and location before treating it as suspicious.

### 3.3 Rapid pass-through and fan-in

Accounts that receive funds from many unrelated senders and forward most of the value within 24 hours exhibit the
classic money-mule pattern. Investigators should identify the ultimate beneficiaries of the outflows and whether
the senders are themselves connected.

### 3.4 Circular flows

Funds that return to the originating account through one or more intermediaries within days, with amounts reduced
only by small fees, indicate possible layering or artificial turnover. Investigators should review the
relationship between all accounts in the cycle.

### 3.5 Dormant reactivation

An account inactive for 90 days or more that suddenly moves significant value should be checked for account
takeover and for misuse of an account sold or rented to a third party.

### 3.6 Geographic anomalies

Card-present transactions in two locations whose distance could not be covered in the elapsed time indicate
possible card cloning. Transactions in a new country should be checked against travel notifications and the
customer's normal travel pattern; travel alone is not suspicious.

### 3.7 Devices and shared identifiers

New devices are common (phone upgrades). A new device becomes significant when combined with a new location,
new beneficiaries or high-value transfers. A device used by three or more customers is a strong network indicator.

### 3.8 High-risk merchants

Escalating payments to gambling, crypto-asset exchange or money-transfer merchants should be compared with the
customer's income and historical spending.

## 4. Network Analysis

Investigators must review the customer's direct transfer counterparties, devices and identifiers. Connections to
customers with open alerts or previously confirmed cases increase priority but do not by themselves establish
suspicion about the subject.

## 5. Documentation Standards

Each investigation file must contain: the subject and scope, the evidence reviewed with references, the risk
indicators observed with their baselines, alternative explanations considered, the uncertainty and missing
information, and the analyst's decision with rationale. See the Investigation Report Standard.

## 6. Service Levels

Standard investigations should be concluded within 20 business days. Investigations involving suspected account
takeover must be prioritised and reviewed the same business day by a senior analyst.
